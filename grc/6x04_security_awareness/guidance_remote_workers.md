# Security Quick Reference: Remote Workers

**[MedDefense Health Systems logo]** | Version 1.0 | 2026-11-18 | Remote administrative, billing and HR staff, and anyone working from home or while traveling

## Your biggest threats

- **Fake MFA and remote-access emails.** "Complete MFA enrollment" or "VPN access review" emails look like the real IT rollout. For remote staff, your VPN login is the attacker's way into the MedDefense network. *Signal: a sign-in link in an email, or an MFA prompt on your phone that you did not start.*
- **Work files drifting into personal space.** Saving a file to personal cloud storage, emailing it to your personal address or printing at home to "make it easier." Patient and employee data then sits outside MedDefense protection, and that can be a reportable HIPAA breach. *Signal: you are about to move a work file somewhere MedDefense does not manage.*
- **Home and public networks and screens.** Shared home WiFi, a router with its default password, coffee-shop WiFi, and people who can see your screen on a train or in a café. *Signal: you can see other people's screens, so they can see yours.*

## What to do right now

- **Connect VPN before opening any work system:** email, billing portal, HRIS or shared drives. Every time.
- Change your home router's default admin password and use WPA2 or WPA3 with a strong WiFi password. Keep the router updated.
- **Keep work and personal devices separate:** use only your MedDefense laptop for work, and do not let family members use it.

## When something looks wrong

**Press Report Phishing in Outlook. For an unexpected MFA prompt, a lost device or a file sent to the wrong place, call ext. 4-SECURITY.** Report it even if you are not sure. It is never a problem.

## What not to do

- Do not use personal email or personal cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud) for work documents, even "just for tonight."
- Do not work on patient or employee information on public WiFi without VPN, or where others can see your screen. Use a privacy screen in public.
- Do not approve an MFA request you did not start, even if it keeps appearing. Deny it and call ext. 4-SECURITY.
