# Security Quick Reference: Clinical Staff

**[MedDefense Health Systems logo]** | Version 1.0 | 2026-11-18 | Nurses, patient care technicians, clinical assistants, charge nurses and supervisors

## Your biggest threats

- **Fake Epic emails at shift start.** Emails that say your Epic access will be held or needs "verification" arrive just before 07:00 or 19:00, when you are logging in and reading handoff. In March 2026, two out of three floor staff who got one clicked it, and that click started a hospital-wide incident. *Watch for: any email asking you to sign in to Epic through a link.*
- **Fake vendor support calls during equipment problems.** A caller says they are from the device or Epic vendor, knows about the problem, and asks you to read a code, install something or give remote access so they can "fix it fast." *Watch for: a caller who rushes you or gives you their own callback number.*
- **USB sticks and shoulder surfing on the unit.** A USB stick left at a nurses' station or a stranger watching your screen at a kiosk can expose patient data and your Epic login. *Watch for: unknown USB devices, and people near your workstation who are not part of the care team.*

## What to do right now

- Open Epic only from your desktop shortcut or the Epic app. Never sign in from an email link, even on your phone.
- Find the **Report Phishing** button in Outlook on your phone and workstation today (Home tab on desktop; **...** > **Report** on mobile).
- Lock the workstation (Windows key + L) or tap out every time you step away, even for one patient.

## When something looks wrong

**Press Report Phishing in Outlook. For calls, USB devices or anything you clicked, call ext. 4-SECURITY.** Reporting after you clicked is still the right thing to do. You will not get in trouble.

## What not to do

- Do not click "verify," "secure message" or "access hold" links because a shift is about to start. Rushing is exactly what the email wants.
- Do not give codes, passwords or remote access to a vendor caller. Hang up and call back using the approved vendor contact list or ask the charge nurse.
- Do not plug in a USB stick you found, even to find its owner. Give it to IT.
