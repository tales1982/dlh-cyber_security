# Security Quick Reference: Executives

**[MedDefense Health Systems logo]** | Version 1.0 | 2026-11-18 | Executive team and executive assistants

## Your biggest threats

- **Business email compromise and wire fraud.** An email in your name, or your CFO's, asks finance to release a confidential payment or change a vendor's bank details today. In Q1 2026, finance executives at three peer organizations received near-identical requests. In March, a "CFO Office" email reached MedDefense finance with replies routed to an outside mailbox. *Signal: urgency, confidentiality and a request not to call.*
- **Spear phishing aimed at you personally.** Board packets, legal matters and acquisition documents are prepared with your real names and calendar. Executives reported none of the four March lures they received. *Signal: a document request that arrives through an unexpected channel or sender.*
- **Travel and public exposure.** Hotel and airport WiFi, an unlocked laptop in a lounge, a conversation overheard on a call. *Signal: you are about to open board or financial material outside a MedDefense network.*

## What to do right now

- Agree with your assistant and the finance team that **any request involving money, credentials or confidential documents is verified by phone or in person**, including requests that appear to come from you.
- Confirm that MFA is enabled on your email and board portal accounts with the IT Service Desk this week.
- Before travel, ask IT for the travel briefing and use VPN for every work session on the road.

## When something looks wrong

**Press Report Phishing in Outlook, then call ext. 4-SECURITY.** If a payment or document has already been sent, call immediately; the first hour decides whether funds can be recovered.

## What not to do

- Do not approve or confirm a payment, banking change or document release by replying to the same email thread.
- Do not ask staff to skip verification because you are busy. Attackers rely on that pressure, and your team must feel free to call you.
- Do not forward board or confidential documents to personal email or open them on shared or public devices.

Your account can authorize operational damage far beyond your inbox. A compromised executive mailbox can be used to order payments, release patient-data contracts and send convincing phishing to the whole organization.
