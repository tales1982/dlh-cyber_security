SCOPE
-----

Between 2026-04-14 and 2026-04-16, MedDefense received a cluster of 8
inbound emails flagged for review.  Three of those emails were determined
to be part of a coordinated phishing campaign.  One nurse (Diane Marsh,
WS-NURSE-04, 10.10.2.15) clicked the link in Email 2 at 2026-04-14
15:02:33 UTC and is assessed to have submitted credentials at 15:02:58.

The 4x00 investigation included:
  - Header analysis of all 8 emails
  - URL and domain investigation for the three phishing emails
  - Authentication header verification (SPF, DKIM, DMARC)
  - WHOIS and passive DNS lookups on campaign domains
  - User impact determination (which accounts potentially exposed)
  - Detection rule development for deployment into Wazuh (rules 100080-100082)
  - Indicator extraction for HC3 submission

The 4x00 investigation did NOT include:
  - Network packet analysis (performed in 4x01)
  - Endpoint forensics on WS-NURSE-04 (performed in 4x01)
  - Follow-on authentication event correlation (performed in 4x01)

Verdict at close of 4x00:  "LIKELY CREDENTIAL EXPOSURE, NO CONFIRMED
                            EXPLOITATION AT TIME OF REPORT"


KEY FINDINGS
------------

F1.  Three emails (E2, E5, E7) share a PHPMailer 6.6.0 X-Mailer header,
     Namecheap-registered lookalike domains, SPF failure (hard fail or
     softfail), DKIM absent, DMARC fail.  The operational pattern is
     consistent across all three.

F2.  The lookalike domains were registered 4-10 days before the first
     email:
       meddefense-portal.com       registered 2026-04-09
       medequip-supplies.net       registered 2026-04-08
       meddefense-benefits.org     registered 2026-04-10

F3.  One email (E3) uses a different technique: a fully-authenticated
     domain (outlook-protection.com) that passes SPF/DKIM/DMARC but
     impersonates Microsoft visually.  The authentication passes because
     the attacker correctly configured DNS for their own lookalike
     domain.  This is a higher-sophistication variant.

F4.  Three MedDefense employees received phishing emails:
       dmarsh    (nurse, Westside Clinic)       CLICKED E2
       arivera   (accounts payable)             did NOT click E5
       lpatterson (billing)                     did NOT click E7

F5.  Credential submission by dmarsh is LIKELY based on:
       - SIEM connection log showing 47-second HTTPS session
       - No concurrent Sysmon Event 11 (no file download)
       - User reported "entered password" after incident disclosure
     but is NOT CONFIRMED via packet evidence as of 4x00 close.

F6.  No immediate post-compromise authentication event observed in the
     dmarsh account on any MedDefense system within the 4x00 window
     (2026-04-14 15:02 through 2026-04-16 16:00).
