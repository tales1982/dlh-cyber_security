TL;DR
-----

Over the past two weeks I have been tracking a phishing operation targeting
US healthcare organizations.  I obtained a copy of the phishing kit by
opening a lure in a sandbox and extracting the server-side PHP from a
misconfigured infrastructure endpoint.  This post walks through the kit,
the operator's infrastructure choices, and -- based on tooling overlap with
campaigns I have documented since 2024 -- what I assess to be the likely
threat actor.  Attribution is MEDIUM confidence and based entirely on
infrastructure and tooling fingerprint.  I am publishing this ahead of
government advisories because several healthcare partners have asked me
to share what I know.

Attribution caveat: I am a solo researcher.  I do not have visibility
into victim telemetry.  Everything in the attribution section is
open-source-derived.  Treat it accordingly.


1.  HOW I FOUND THE KIT
-----------------------

On 2026-04-18 a contact at a Midwest hospital forwarded me a phishing
email.  The URL was:

    https://meddefense-portal.com/verify/staff?id=<REDACTED>&token=a8f3e2d1

I visited the URL from an isolated analysis VM.  The landing page was a
MedDefense-themed credential form, but the page also referenced a static
asset path (/kit/static/) that resolved to a directory listing.  The
operator had forgotten to disable autoindex.  From that directory I was
able to walk up one level and recover the entire kit: index.php, form
handlers, config.php, and a TXT file that appears to be the operator's
install log.

The kit was still live at time of writing but was taken down around
2026-04-22.


2.  KIT STRUCTURE
-----------------

The kit is a PHP-based single-page credential harvester:

    index.php            Landing page (customized per target org)
    handlers/post.php    Accepts POSTed form data, writes to logs/creds.log
                         and forwards via PHPMailer to an attacker-controlled
                         mailbox hosted on outlook-protection.com.
    static/              Static assets (JS, CSS, logos) spoofing each
                         target brand.
    config.php           Central config. Relevant values:

        MAILER_VERSION = "PHPMailer 6.6.0"
        SMTP_HOST      = "smtp.<harvester-domain>"
        TARGET_LOG     = "/var/www/kit/logs/creds.log"
        OPS_CONTACT    = "ops@healthbane-c2.net"      <-- !!
        EXFIL_ENDPOINT = "https://healthbane-c2.net/api/ingest"

That single reference to `healthbane-c2.net` is what bridges Stage 1 and
Stage 2/3 infrastructure from my perspective.  Until I saw that config
file, Stage 1 domains (meddefense-portal.com et al.) could plausibly have
been a separate operator.

Logos and text in static/ are pulled from the real target websites,
suggesting the operator uses a per-target templating script -- not manual
customization.  That is a quality indicator: mass production.

4.  TOOLING
-----------

The kit consistently uses:

    * PHPMailer 6.6.0   for outbound SMTP (hardcoded X-Mailer header)
    * wkhtmltopdf 0.12.6  for PDF lure generation (producer metadata)
    * Let's Encrypt certificates issued 1-7 days before first use
    * Namecheap registrar for all phishing-LP domains
    * Njalla registrar for operator domains (healthbane-c2.net)

The wkhtmltopdf version string is distinctive -- I have flagged this as
a high-fidelity operational signature.  0.12.6 is not the latest (0.12.7
was released 2023) but it is the version that ships with many cheap
"PDF generation as a service" containers, and this operator is re-using
one.

I pulled three sample PDFs from the kit's static/ directory.  All three
had the wkhtmltopdf 0.12.6 producer field and embedded URLs pointing to
one of the HEALTHBANE-affiliated domains.

6.  ATTRIBUTION (MEDIUM CONFIDENCE)
-----------------------------------

This is where I depart from what HC3 will publish.  My assessment:

The kit fingerprint (PHPMailer 6.6.0 + Njalla registration of operator
domains + config.php structure with `EXFIL_ENDPOINT` / `OPS_CONTACT`
keys) matches three previous campaigns I documented in 2024 and 2025:

  * 2024-07  "RXBRIDGE" campaign against US pharmacies
  * 2024-11  "CLAIMBRIDGE" campaign against insurance processors
  * 2025-09  "MEDNEXUS" campaign against hospital billing vendors

All three used the same kit structure with only target-branding changes.
Domains were registered at Namecheap with DigitalOcean/Hostinger/OVH
hosting.  Operator domains used Njalla.  Exfil endpoints followed the
pattern `<word>-c2.net`.

I track this operator privately as APT-MEDAGENT.  I do not know if this
is a named group internally at any commercial vendor.  I have seen one
commercial feed (Acme) use the label "VITALSCORE" for overlapping
activity.  I have no visibility into whether Acme's VITALSCORE
corresponds 1:1 with my APT-MEDAGENT.

Confidence: MEDIUM, based entirely on tooling + infrastructure overlap.
NOT based on telemetry, signals intelligence, or insider reporting.

Note on the commercial label VITALSCORE:
    The Acme commercial feed uses this label for what is clearly the
    same operational activity.  However, Acme's clustering model
    incorporates ML-based similarity on low-evidence indicators and
    generates false positives.  I would treat the VITALSCORE label as
    an operator ALIAS but would not over-weight their additional
    indicators that lack corroboration.


7.  DEFENSIVE NOTES
-------------------

If you are defending a healthcare organization, the highest-value
detections for this operator are operational pattern based:

    (1)  Newly registered domains at Namecheap with healthcare keywords
         combined in compound form (`meddefense-*`, `*-portal`,
         `*-benefits`, `med*-supplies`).

    (2)  PHPMailer 6.6.0 in inbound email X-Mailer headers, combined
         with SPF failure.  This is a high-fidelity single-string
         indicator for this operator.

    (3)  DNS queries for `*-c2.net` lookalikes at the zone level.

Indicator-based blocking (domains/IPs) will work for about one week.
Operational-pattern detections will survive rotation.
