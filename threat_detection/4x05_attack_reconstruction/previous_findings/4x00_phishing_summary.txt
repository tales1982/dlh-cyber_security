PURPOSE OF THIS DOCUMENT
------------------------
This file is a consolidated reference summary of the 4x00 phishing
dissection.  It is NOT the original report.  The original triage notes,
header parses, sandboxed URL output and per-email worksheets remain
in the 4x00 evidence package.  This summary extracts the findings that
the 4x05 reconstruction will need to cross-reference: campaign
classification, IOCs, credential exposure, detection rules deployed,
and evidence reliability assessment.

CAMPAIGN CLASSIFICATION
-----------------------
  Campaign name:           HEALTHBANE  (assigned by HHS HC3 in
                           advisory HC3-2026-HEALTHBANE-001)
  Campaign type:           Targeted credential harvesting + malware
                           delivery, healthcare-sector focus
  Operator profile:        Financially motivated, English-language

THE THREE CONFIRMED MALICIOUS EMAILS
------------------------------------

[E1]  Subject:  "Action required: update your MedDefense portal
                  password before 2026-04-15"
      Sender:   no-reply@meddefense-portal.com  (lookalike domain)
      Spoof:    Display name "MedDefense IT" -- not the legitimate
                no-reply@meddefense.local internal sender
      Recipients: 14 MedDefense addresses (clinical + records)
      Body:     HTML + tracking pixel.  Single CTA link to
                  https://meddefense-portal.com/login.aspx
      Header analysis:
        Return-Path:   bounce@meddefense-portal.com
        SPF:           hardfail (sender IP not in SPF for portal)
        DKIM:          missing (no signature)
        DMARC:         fail (no alignment)
        Received:      Originating IP 91.219.236.117 (Russia, VPS)
                       6 hops, last MTA at LeaseWeb DE
      Sandbox:  https://meddefense-portal.com/login.aspx renders
                an Office 365 credential-harvesting form.  POST goes
                to /collect.php on same host.  TLS cert: Let's Encrypt
                issued 2026-04-12 (matches WHOIS registration date).

[E2]  Subject:  "Final notice: HR benefits enrollment closes today"
      Sender:   benefits@meddefense-benefits.com  (lookalike)
      Recipients: 9 MedDefense addresses (mostly clinical)
      Body:     PDF attachment with embedded link
                  https://meddefense-benefits.com/enroll
      Header analysis:
        SPF:           hardfail
        DKIM:          missing
        DMARC:         fail
        Received:      Originating IP 45.142.214.108 (DE, same VPS
                       provider as E1)
      Sandbox:  Identical credential capture page styled with
                MedDefense branding lifted from the public website.

[E3]  Subject:  "Your Microsoft 365 mailbox is over quota"
      Sender:   no-reply@outlook-protection.com  (generic Microsoft
                impersonation)
      Recipients: 22 MedDefense addresses
      Body:     CTA "Verify your account"
      Header analysis:
        SPF:           hardfail
        DKIM:          missing
        DMARC:         fail
      Sandbox:  Generic Microsoft credential phishing page.  Lower
                campaign confidence (the same pattern appears in
                many unrelated campaigns) but timing and recipient
                set match HEALTHBANE.

KEY USER EVENT : DIANE MARSH CLICK
----------------------------------
At 2026-04-14T13:18:05Z (08:18 CDT), Diane Marsh on WS-RECV-03
(records department, account dmarsh) opened email E1 and clicked
the credential link.  Browser history confirmed the resolution to
meddefense-portal.com/login.aspx.  At 13:18:42Z she submitted her
domain credentials.  PCAP from 4x01 captured the POST.

Post-click user behavior:
  - Diane noticed the page did not match the internal portal
    (different colors) and reported it to the helpdesk at 13:35Z.
  - Robert Kim rotated dmarsh's password at 13:20Z (within 2 minutes
    of her report routing through internal IM, before the formal
    helpdesk ticket was filed).
  - dmarsh's session was force-revoked in Azure AD at 13:22Z.

This 17-minute window between credential capture and rotation is the
operational gap exploited by HEALTHBANE.  The attacker had ~17 minutes
to use the dmarsh credential before AD rotated it.  4x01 will show
that the attacker did not act on dmarsh directly inside that window
(no logon-as-dmarsh observed at MedDefense after the rotation), but
4x04 evidence suggests the attacker pivoted to a different credential
acquisition path (LSASS dump on the already-compromised WS-RECV-03
host).

DETECTION RULES DEPLOYED
------------------------
After 4x00 the following rules entered production:

  [R1]  Mail gateway: hold-and-quarantine on any inbound mail with
        SPF hardfail + DKIM missing + sender domain registered
        within previous 30 days (matches HEALTHBANE delivery
        pattern).
  [R2]  Wazuh custom rule 100080: alert on any DNS query or HTTP
        request to meddefense-portal.com / meddefense-benefits.com
        / outlook-protection.com from any internal host.
  [R3]  Wazuh custom rule 100081: alert on any URL click in
        Microsoft Defender for Office 365 click-trail telemetry
        targeting the same domain set.

These three rules generated the persistent low-volume background
of HEALTHBANE alerts that was visible throughout the 4x04 hunt
window (rule 100080 fires several times per day across many
workstations -- all from cached browser tabs, RSS readers, etc.,
not from active credential submission).

CREDENTIAL EXPOSURE ASSESSMENT
------------------------------

                        Reason: direct evidence of submission to
                        attacker-controlled URL
                        Mitigation: AD password rotated 13:20Z,
                        all sessions revoked
  Possibly exposed:     Up to 7 other recipients of E1/E2/E3 who
                        could not confirm whether they clicked.
                        4x00 advised AD password reset for all 45
                        recipients out of caution; this was completed
                        2026-04-15.
  NOT exposed:          Service accounts (svc_*) -- these are not
                        in Azure AD and could not have been phished.
                        4x04 finding that svc_healthsync was abused
                        is a separate compromise vector (LSASS dump
                        on WS-RECV-03), not a phishing outcome.

OPEN QUESTIONS HANDED TO 4x01
-----------------------------

       rotation, and to authenticate to what ?
  Q2.  Did the credential-harvesting POST succeed (i.e., were her
       credentials transmitted in cleartext to the attacker) or
       did it fail TLS, JS, etc. ?
  Q3.  Were any of the malicious URLs reachable from any other
       MedDefense host besides WS-RECV-03 ?

(4x01 answered Q1 and Q2.  Q3 remained open until 4x04 confirmed
that other workstations did query the C2 root domain
healthbane-c2.net, indicating that the malware Stage 2 succeeded
on hosts beyond WS-RECV-03 -- but the credential-capture URLs
themselves were only hit from WS-RECV-03.)

NOTES FOR 4x05 RECONSTRUCTION
-----------------------------
  - The 4x00 evidence is the temporal anchor for the start of the
    HEALTHBANE attack against MedDefense.  Use 2026-04-14T13:18:42Z
    as the credential-submission timestamp and 13:20Z as the
    rotation timestamp.
  - dmarsh credentials were rotated in 17 minutes; the attacker
    therefore did NOT establish persistence using dmarsh's credential
    after Stage 1.  The fact that WS-RECV-03 became a long-term
    pivot host is explained by Stage 2 (RAT delivery via Stage 1
    payload) and Stage 4 (LSASS dump for service credential), not
    by ongoing dmarsh credential reuse.
  - The 4x00 sandbox confirmed credential capture but did NOT
    capture any post-credential redirect or follow-on payload
    delivery from the harvest page.  If the attacker delivered the
    Stage 2 dropper through the same harvest interaction, that
    delivery is invisible in 4x00 evidence (sandbox rendered the
    form and submitted dummy credentials, did not follow the post-
    submit chain).  The 4x03 dropper sample was recovered from a
    different vector: a follow-up email with .docm attachment sent
    to the same recipient pool 24h after the harvest emails.
