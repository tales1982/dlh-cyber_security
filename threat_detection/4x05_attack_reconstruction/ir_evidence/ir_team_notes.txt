HOW TO READ THESE NOTES
=======================
Lines tagged [HIGH] are observations the author considers high-confidence
based on direct evidence in hand at the time of writing.

Lines tagged [MED] are observations the author believes likely but has
not personally verified or that depend on another team member's claim.

Lines tagged [LOW] are speculation, hypothesis, or someone's verbal claim
that has NOT been documented elsewhere and should be tested before
inclusion in any external-facing artifact.

Lines tagged [DISPUTED] are observations where two team members disagree;
the disagreement is documented for the reconstruction analyst to resolve.

Lines tagged [TODO] are things the author intended to follow up on but
had not as of the last edit.

ENTRY #006  -- 2026-05-16  11:30 CDT
Author: Mike Torres
Subject: FIRST PASS AT EXFIL VOLUME
================================================================================
[HIGH] In the firewall log I see THREE distinct outbound bursts to
       185.220.101.45:443 that are MUCH larger than the normal 5-min
       beacon (~ 1 KB out).  Sizes:
         2026-05-08T07:38Z  -- 14 219 484 bytes out
         2026-05-11T08:17Z  -- 11 802 944 bytes out
         2026-05-13T07:34Z  --  8 419 232 bytes out
       Total: 34 441 660 bytes (~ 32.8 MB).
[MED]  These bursts are too small to be a full clinical-records dump if
       the attacker grabbed everything (estimated 47k patient records
       with 6 columns ~ 14 MB compressed -- so the FIRST burst could
       very well BE the full patient table after gzip).  Working
       hypothesis: the attacker exfiltrated specific tables, compressed,
       and uploaded.  Disk forensics will tell us what's in each
       zip if Autopsy can carve them.
[HIGH] In addition: between 2026-05-05 and 2026-05-15 there is ELEVATED
       BYTES_OUT in the daily KNOWN_C2 sessions on two days specifically
       (5/5 and 5/12) that DOES sum to ~ 23 MB each day.  Those are
       the days of the LSASS dumps.  I think those days carried the
       LSASS dump output up over many small beacons rather than a
       single burst.  This makes the dump exfil HARDER to detect with
       a "look for one big upload" rule.

[ANALYST -- VERY IMPORTANT for HIPAA scoping]
The cleanest exfil-volume number we have is 32.8 MB across 3 bursts.
The disk-recovered ZIPs (14.2 + 11.8 + 8.4 MB) sum to 34.4 MB.  These
are the SAME bytes -- the firewall sees compressed-on-wire size and
the disk artifacts are uncompressed-on-disk-then-recompressed-into-zip
size, but they should match within ~ 5% if my reading is right.
14 219 484 bytes shows up in BOTH the firewall log and the disk MFT
record for staging_export_001.zip -- exact match.  That's a smoking
gun for "this archive was uploaded to this C2 at this time."
