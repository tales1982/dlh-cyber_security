## Could MedDefense pass a HIPAA security audit today?

**No.** Every requirement in the table above shows either an outright "No" or, at best, "Partially" compliant, and the evidence supporting each finding is not speculative it traces directly to confirmed 1x02 vulnerability scan findings and this project's own T0 Data Protection Map, meaning an auditor would not need to take MedDefense's word for the gap; the evidence already exists in MedDefense's own prior assessment documentation. **The single most critical encryption deficiency an auditor would cite is the complete absence of at-rest encryption for the patient database itself (§164.312(a)(2)(iv))** while transmission security gaps are serious, an auditor evaluating overall risk to ePHI would very likely treat "the entire patient database sits on an unencrypted disk, readable in full by anyone who obtains root or physically removes the drive" as the single highest-severity finding, both because it is the most direct, largest-blast-radius path to a reportable breach of the greatest volume of PHI, and because it is the one gap on this table where MedDefense cannot even claim a partial mitigating control is in place (unlike authentication, where AD at least provides some baseline). Notably, this is not new information invented for this audit T0's Data Protection Map identified this exact gap in this project's very first task, and the fact that it remains unremediated through this entire cryptography project's fifteen prior findings-and-recommendations tasks is itself the kind of pattern (a documented, known gap left open for an extended period) that HIPAA enforcement actions have specifically penalized more severely than a gap an organization simply had not yet discovered.
